1. INTRO

The idea for this char was born when I turned my Vengeance Summoner Sorc into a Winter’s Gambit Sorc with Shard of the North Star. When it worked well, I just went “eh, I should make a Barb instead since the skill is his and I don’t have one made”. A lot of items and ideas are shared between the chars.

(The funniest part for me - yes, it takes that little to amuse me - is the diametrical flavor going when comparing these 2 builds: the Sorc is the prime caster char, but there she is a melee; while Barb is the prime melee char, but here he is a ranged dude. Sorc is mostly fire-based with a cold proc; Barb is mostly cold-based with a fire proc. Shes the viking barbie; hes the viking chad.)

Cold throw barb was a bit on the downside since Cold Mastery was removed from Splinterfreeze (this one wasn’t on me, I swear xD) and it directly competes with “rock thrower” Barbs, that have more power level right out of the bat and also procs a lot of CTC effects. The introduction of Cold Enchant on the A3 mercs though did a lot to help out the build.

As anyone should notice by now, I just like shoving cool items into already functional builds, and that is the case here with using a Ragnarok helm. 

The druid wolvies are mostly a distraction tactic and something to make using Winter’s Gambit easier and not absolute damage machines even with cold enchant (the cold ones dish some very considerable damage, but the fire ones are on the weak side), but I thought that is very complimentary for the build and it comes at a very low cost at the endgame when you have Black Soulstone and Obsidian Beacon making Ragnarok stat requirements a lot tamer to achieve.

If you are not into the whole Wolf summon thing though and prefer not having them you can just swap a couple of items (mostly helm, gloves and jewelry selection) and that will neat an amazing 3k-ish extra damage return (oooooh wow much damage).

3. REASONS TO PLAY THIS GAMBIT BARBARIAN

  1. Damage. The build scales a lot regarding damage, even if it starts on the lower side and doesn’t compare favorably to the physical-scaling throw skills at the early stages. Once you start to amass projectile numbers and a lot of pierce (both in the real sense as in the enemy resistance sense), you start to kill things a lot faster, with bosses just completely MELTING.
  2. You look cool while doing it.
  3. You are a barbarian, meaning you get almost every stat under the sun ‘free’ in some amounts, which makes the process of gearing while playing very enjoyable and easy.

 

4. REASONS TO NOT PLAY THIS GAMBIT BARBARIAN

  1. Winter’s Gambit itself. The skill might be not everybody cup of tea. The spell has an initial thow range that has a deadzone where it doesn’t hit anything, and then after it reaches that max range (or hits some obstacle), its projectiles will then seek the closer enemy in a determined range, meaning they tend to very often converge into a single one instead of spreading. That is particularly triggering at the starting stages since your damage output is low, but once you start to amassing more projectiles, ias, pierce and just gets more used to the skill; these drawbacks start to lessen. With the addition of wolves running distraction in the late, it gets even easier. But the road might be not that pleasant for everyone, so you gotta play to see if the skill is for you or not.

 

5. MAIN STATS

Regular decades-old D2 stat logic. The bare minimum you need to equip gear on STR, whatever you feel you need on DEX for attack rating (and block too) and everything else on VIT.

Specially since Ragnarok Helm has such high requirements to equip (even at good increased requirements rolls), you will juggle a lot of stat points around during the game. At the end, with Torches and other Unique charms (Ogre King in particular), you can get away with low STR, but that is not the case for most of the time you will spend playing.

 

6. ENDGAME SKILL BUILD

During game progression, you can swap stuff around as you feel useful, with extra points into Throw Mastery helping quite a bit at the start due to extra piercing.

For lvl 100 with all extra skill points, this is the build to use:

  • 25 Winter’s Gambit (Your main vehicle of damage. The most important part about leveling this skill though is accruing more projectiles, and not necessarily its added damage, that is not particularly impressive share of your damage until very late in the game)
  • 25 Korlic’s Fury (While leveling, will be the main source of cold damage. Not because its that good, just because it is a bit more than Winter’s Gambit xD)
  • 25 Unstoppable Force (Synergy for cold damage, but during leveling will be your tool to kill Cold Immunes before you can get a Sunder Charm).
  • 24 Chasm Break (Synergy. 25 points only if you forego one of the 1-pointers)
  • 1 Throwing Mastery (At the end, you will get 50% extra pierce by just having one point, and the other benefits it give are not particularly good to you as your base weapon damage will be low. During leveling I did spend some more points to get some extra pierce though.)
  • 1 Shield Mastery (Lessens the numbers of DEX needed to get good block chance and better block rate.)
  • 1 Increased speed (Zoomies)
  • 1 Iron Skin (‘Free’ armor)
  • 1 Natural Skin (Barb Lyfe 2ezz)
  • 1 Howl (Pre-req)
  • 1 Shout (‘Free’ armor)
  • 1 Battle Orders (Every Barb bread and butter. At early stages spend as many points as you find necessary and can afford here if you think you need more life and mana or just plainly find it a chore to keep the buff up at low skill levels. At the endgame though 1 point will be enough. You don’t take that much damage since you are a ranged char, and you will have a lot of life just by being a barbarian and you will have wolvies soaking damage for you too).
  • 1 Battle Command (‘Free’ skill level)
  • 1 Find Potion (Pre-req. I would honestly skip if using a Deadly Treasures Large Charm of you don’t care about the MF passive)
  • 1 Find Item (Pre-req and nice tool to have. I would honestly skip if using a Deadly Treasures Large Charm of you don’t care about the MF passive. Never felt too much of a need to hork that much in the mod).
  • 1 Deadly Treasures (A lot of ‘free’ MF at the end. Can safely be skip If you don’t care about it)

That would be exactly 111 skill points.

 

7. GEAR SETUP 

The build is very gear dependent to be competitive at later levels (and that gear requirement will involve your mercenary as well), and leveling with it wont be a ‘free’ endeavor like the throw-rock skills are, but its really not hard nor slow if you keep your gear up to date and understand its mechanics.

 

7.1. HELM SLOT – RAGNAROK

The endgame slot for this item is Ragnarok. It gives you fanaticism and 5 oSkill on both druid wolves. When rolling for this item, I would benefit low rolls on increased requirement stat over fanaticism level, but at the end of the day I would pick the item based on the best corruption unless you get absurd levels of increased requirements. 

On the screenshot example I didn’t even bother to corrupt mine, but just use the best one you can get.

For upgrades, 3x 2 cold skills is the best one. It doesn’t benefit the fire wolves, but those are mostly just damage sponges anyway. The cold skills will be a lot more important, specially to get to the 12 projectile cap (lvl 54 Winter’s Gambit needed) and also boost your cold wolfs and Korlic Fury a lot more.

7.1.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR HELM

The thing about Ragnarok is that I absolute do not recommend using it before having a good amount of +all skills. The preferred spot I found was after killing Tristram Ubers and fully upgrading Anni and Torch for those +ask and bonus stats. Before that, your wolves will just die too much, you will feel the absence of Nightwing’s 2 all skills a lot, you might not actually have another source of CBF yet and your stat will feel too spread around making your life numbers kinda pitiful.

Therefore, I recommend using a Nightwing’s Veil until that point. It is generic 2ask, it has CBF and it boosts your Cold Damage quite a lot. That helm will be your go-to for most of the content, and it is on the same base to Conversion Orb for as Ragnarok, so you are bound to get some that you can corrupt and use a lot, while you fish for a Ragnarok with good stats and corrupts. Its a pretty cool gearing dynamic for that stage of the game.

Another very good option made available at the last patch is using 2 pieces of M’avina’s set. Using the diadem + belt will grand you (among other very good stats) 1 all skills, 1 cold skills and -25% enemy cold resist. I used it a lot during leveling and early post game farm as set items are very easy to get since Assemblage Orbs usually are more available as your Conversion orbs are tied trying to get the more urgent upgrades.

 

7.2. GLOVE SLOT – RUPTUROUS BLESSING

This was my choice of gloves for several reasons: I planned on using Corruption Coils set at the end so I knew I needed a lot of resists to offset that set of jewelry, even without that the overcap on resist was super useful to face Uber Ancients, and the resist aura helped the wolves to stay alive A LOT. You can upgrade it to Vambrances since you will have more than enough Str to use it, even if the armor gain is low (they are Heavy Bracers base).

The best corruption on this slot is -5 all res or 5% elemental damage and 10FCR. I wouldn’t care about Iron Skin corrupt since you are a Barb. 

For enchantments I went with 3x 6% pierce, but see how much you need to not overcap it.

7.2.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR GLOVES

Other options for this slot would be elemental crafts (theoretical best and a must if you are not going for the Summner angle), or a 2ask Lachdanan’s (pretty good giving all skills, attack rating and some more stats), or Frostburns if noting better is available.

 

7.3. ARMOR SLOT – ARKAINE’S VALOR

This one is the best options barring some bonker crafts (that have a high threshold to clear to be better than Arkaine’s so I wouldn’t even bother).

It gives you a ton of life, and a lot of damage on higher rolls.

For corruption, almost everything is useful, with the 1-percenter all skills being the better one.

7.3.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR CHEST ARMOR

Other good options here are Cold Comfort, Iceskin and Ocean’s Embrace – all of them having its reasons to be picked over the other contextually depending on the rest of your gear. Ocean’s Embrace is funny though because it has level requirement absurdly higher than the other 2, but barely more damage output and no noticeable defensive boost, especially when compared to Iceskin. Maybe this one could use a buff or something xD

 

7.4. BELT SLOT – ECSTASY OF ISHTAR

This slot was the one that gave me the most pierce at the lowest opportunity cost comparing to the rest of the gear, so that’s why I went for it.

This slot can also corrupt to extra pierce, so if you get that it might also factor in your choice of enchants and gear selection on the glove slot. I got a 1 all skills corrupt, so I kept this 27% Pierce ‘only’ copy because of that.

Enchant here I would choose 3x 6 stats 6 res because stats help equipping some more gear and give some good benefits, but I didn’t come around to do it before taking the screenshot.

 7.4.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR BELT

Good old Razortail is also a completely acceptable item for this slot if you don't wanna have to deal with Ishtar's variable pierce % rolls. It is constantly better than a Ishtar on average unless your Ishtar is on the good side of rolls.

Other option here is just slapping an elemental crafted or some cold damage leaning belt (like Snowclash) and work extra pierce in some other way, but I don't find that anywhere close to optimal.

Lastly, specially during leveling you can use M'avina's belt here for 1 cold skills and a bunch of cold resist pierce if used with another set piece.

 

7.5. BOOT SLOT – CRAFTED ELEMENTAL

This is one of the 2 slots I didn’t feel good scaping from this damned elemental crafts. There is no other feel-good boot to use here, even though you can make it completely fine with. This can be a good slot to get some Burst of Speed charges early on to help with your IAS breakpoints, but by the end you really don’t need it to cap that, so I returned the BoS pair I stole from my amazon to her and used these with max resists instead.

No hard requirements on corruption here, and same thing for enchants. I guess the theoretical best enchants would be %life, but I felt Str/Vit was just a sliver worse numbers-wise but it allowed me to equip Ragnarok easier.

7.5.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR BOOTS

Zero Effect are the only other boot I would consider using here, but all the chonkyness really doesn’t feel necessary currently.

Lustwanderers can be very attractive before you get your Anni and upgrade it.

For damage, a sub-in for elemental crafts would be Asheara's Slippers, but its honestly just worse than Elemental Crafts, specially before you reach 54 skill points into Winter’s Gambit.

 

7.6. WEAPON SLOT – FALLEN GLORY

There are some higher damage options for this slot (including the always boring Elemental Crafts), but my weapon of choice here was Fallen Glory. It has a lot of +skill potential that you can put to use to max out projectile numbers early and a proc that actually does a lot of damage. 

The best corruption on this slot is -5 resist +5 elemental damage (with a FCR variant close behind), but I didn’t come around to try to get one on mine. 

Upgrades, doing 1 flat elemental 63-511 would shake up to be more damage, and for the other 4 I guess the most min-max would be 4x -2/2 cold enchants. I did 5x cold ones though cuz I forgot xD.

With its innate 20% ias, you will need a lvl9 Fanaticism to reach 10 ias frames. That is 1frame away from the 9 FPA cap on this weapon. I found that to be good enough and didn’t care to go for more, but you will get 10% more IAS on your Queen’s Call, and if you can scrap 14 more (like with a Triskelion or double ring upgrades), you can reach the 9IAS bp with the same level of Fanaticism. For lower levels though, you will have to find even more sources of IAS, so use a calculator and judge by yourself where you can squeeze in the numbers from.

Regarding the weapon proc, in theory Winter’s Gabit is marked to only proc stuff on the middle 2 projectiles, but in practice, it just feels a lot more. Its hard to say it really procs on more than that or if its just impression due to high pierce numbers and whatnot, but you just get falling meteors all the time.

7.6.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR WEAPONS

If not using Fallen Glory, the the only thing I’d consider in its place is a good Elemental Craft.

 

7.7. OFF-HAND SLOT – ELEMENTAL CRAFT

This slot is the second one I went for an elemental craft shield. 

Damage-wise you could even do a Phase-Blade craft instead of a shield, since that would be 2 more jewel slots than the shield and crafted weapons can also roll 1-2 Barbarian or 1-3 Masteries & Throwing skills and enchant it for 10% extra cold damage and -10 enemy cold resist and corrupt for extra 5 of those stats too; which would put a craft like this MILES ahead of a shield in terms of damage output. 

For me though it just felt plain WRONG to do it, so I didn’t haha. It would also remove your ability to block, which might or might not be something very desirable in the future if harder content is added to the game (for as it current is, you would be totally ok though, so keep the option in mind).

For corrupts, anything is useful.

For enchants, I would for sure go with 5x 3 all resists/2 max resists (if using the shield route).

7.7.1. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS FOR OFF-HAND

Other options here could be a second pair of Fallen Glory for more skills and damage-leaning enchants and corrupts for the same reasons a Phase-Blade craft could be preferred over a Shield craft. They wont proc even more Starfalls as CTCs are localized and Gambit doesn’t attack with the off-hand weapon, but that doesn’t matter much.

Other option is Cry of the Wretched. It has IAS and FCR, as well as 1 all skill and Lower Resist on strike and on struck procs.

Finally, Chill of Winter is also another option if want more Procs with your Procs, but tis really underwhelming IMO.

 

7.8. JEWELRY SLOTS

7.8.1. PRE LVL 100 GEARING

Honestly, pre-lvl 100 just use whatever good rings and amulet you can find. These are pretty good slots to shore up resist shortage, getting CBF and other stats you need at this point.

I used a Sigil of Hope on neck slot because its just SO GOOD. 7.8.2. LVL 100 – CORRUPTION COILS SET

Once I reached lvl 100, I went with the Corruption Coil set. This set does require you to skew your gearing a lot to accommodate for the drawbacks and the lack of stats the set has, but if you can do that, they are super good. Specially if you can leverage the %elemental bonus in multiple fronts, or in some imbalanced way. This build was super good towards achieving those goals, due to how well it could accommodate Rupturous Blessing into the glove slot, and how particular good the 40% extra damage affects your wolves and your Starfall procs.

The neck corrupt best besides 1ask is -5resist/5% damage 10FCR, but I didn’t bother to go for it on this one yet (lots of room to improve this char, as you can see).

For enchants, neck is locked into Barb skills. Rings you can make whatever you need to tie up holes in your gear, like stats or ias

7.11. CHARMS

Honestly, all unique charms are good here. Every single one of them give you good benefits, and the +skill ones are particularly good, alongside Annihilus and Obsidian Beacon. Do not skip on Conclave of Elements.

9. GAMEPLAY LOOP

Regular gameplay loop consists of:

  • Swap to alternate gear.
  • Buff Battle Command.
  • cast your dogs and do whatever buffs you have with you.
  • Buff Battle Command again, Battle Orders, Shout on yourself, your merc and all wolves.
  • Swap back to main setup.
  • Go out into the wild and kill stuff.

Theres considerable setup time, but very low maintanance after you stack a bunch of + skills.

 

10. MECHANICS

10.1. WINTER’S GAMBIT

As alluded to earlier, Winter’s Gambit works in not a traditional way to most “multishot” ranged skills. 

Its first ‘cone’ is actually a deadzone that wont hit anything on its way. Then, after travelling the required distance, or hitting some other obstacle in the way, it will spawn new missiles that will seek the closer target in a set range. These new missiles are the ones that can pierce and proc effects, then hitting new targets on its path if they happen to be there.

This means that if mobs close their gap and engage you in melee, throwing the skill on their direction wont hit them on the first cone. If theres nothing else close, they will revert back and go to the mobs in your melee since the seek range is bigger than the starting cone length, but if there are other mobs at range that are closer, they will go for those mobs, and you wont be killing the mobs that are whacking at you in melee range.

This can lead to some frustration playing the skill, specially at the early levels IMO. Sometimes its even better to target a wall behind you than throwing the skill ahead of you, if you want to kill a mob that got too close. A bunch of funky stuff can be used to do ‘corner tricks’ and maximize the spell. 

You just have to be patience and learn how to use it.

 

10.2. KORLIC’S FURY

A quick mention on this spell is that it doesn’t work exactly as you would expect for this kind of spell after you get used to D2 mechanics. The spell is a shapeshift, and it grants a flat amount of cold damage added to the attack of the user. At first glance you would imagine this would double ping with cold mastery, multiplying the tooltip damage again by the numbers of your extra %cold damage on gear. This does not happen with this skill though.

I didn’t check the files to see if this is because the tooltip descriptions already take the double dip into consideration on its calculations, or if this doesn’t happen because of some other mechanical layering I’m not aware of - but enough to just mention that the tooltip damage shown is exactly what you get with this one, differently from cold enchant (as it will be mentioned below).

 

10.3. ENCHANT

Enchant has multiple layers of “stuff” going on. 

Enchant double dips masteries bonus. What is meant by that? Every source of elemental damage that is applied to a character that can attack is multiplied once by the WIELDER'S %damage on those elements. These sources also get multiplied first by similar bonuses on the CASTER'S gear beforehand, and if you are both the Caster and the Wielder, thas when you have the double dip.

This means for this build that the mercenary's enchant number, whatever it is, when applied to you, also gets multiplied again. So, your if your merc is a cold variant, when he enchants you for lets say 5k cold damage, and you have 200% cold damage on your gear, you get 15k cold damage added to your attacks.

This is what cranks the build damage numbers and the reason why you SHOULD to have an A3 cold merc with you. My current not enchanted Winter’s Gambit max damage is currently 17k; with my merc enchant bumping it to 31k.

 

10.4. SUMMON WOLF

Summon wolves are particular regarding most summons in that it has imbued in its damage calculations the caster’s %fire and cold skill damage.

As far as I understood checking the files, that just straight multiply the numbers of the wolves normal attack and CTC spells, and not actually give the wolves %elemental damage modifiers (differently from -enemy res, which they actually inherit the stat). 

That basically means that when they are enchanted by the merc’s skill, they dont multiply those values again like the player would as explained on the last chapter - the enchant will affect them only by what is written on the source's tooltip.

Still, %elemental damage is beneficial for them in that way and its the reason approaching even a zoo druid as an elemental caster instead of just +ask stacking is better. Just wanted to mention it somewhere in case someone didn’t know.
